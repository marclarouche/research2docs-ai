Part 4. How to demonstrate the finished project
The strength of Research2Docs AI as a portfolio piece will be demonstrating not just that it can generate documents, but that it applies technical writing discipline.
I recommend creating a portfolio demonstration using a real, publicly available cybersecurity reference.
For example, use the NIST AI Risk Management Framework as the primary source material.
With that single research collection, demonstrate the system producing content for three different audiences.
Output	Target audience	Demonstrated skill
What Is the NIST AI RMF?	General public	Simplification of complex technical concepts
Implementing AI Risk Management	Cybersecurity professionals	Technical explanation and instructional writing
AI Governance: Executive Overview	Organizational leadership	Audience adaptation and content strategy
Then demonstrate the evidence review system with one deliberately unsupported statement.
The application should flag the claim, explain that the source material does not support it, and allow a reviewer to inspect the original passage.
That sequence provides a concrete demonstration of editorial judgment, not just AI-generated writing.
Final portfolio positioning
Research2Docs AI should complement KnowledgeBridge AI rather than duplicate it.
KnowledgeBridge AI	Research2Docs AI
Answers questions from documentation	Produces new documentation from research
Focuses on knowledge retrieval	Focuses on content transformation
Returns cited answers	Produces cited documents
Identifies unsupported answers	Maintains claim-level verification records
Demonstrates knowledge management	Demonstrates technical writing and editorial strategy
My recommendation is to build the complete v1 around these five content types, with claim-level source traceability as the defining feature. Avoid expanding into automated web research or publishing until the foundational evidence and editorial workflow is reliable.
Once completed, this will give the five-project portfolio a particularly valuable final example: an application that demonstrates how you research technical material, evaluate evidence, adapt complex information, and make responsible editorial decisions.
